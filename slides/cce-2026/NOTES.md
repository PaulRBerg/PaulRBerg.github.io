How to explain the rise of China
  The one-line answer

  ▎ China is the URM's most interesting test case — and on inspection it confirms, not refutes, the thesis.

  The four moves, in order

  1. Catch-up ≠ frontier. China's 40-year ascent is overwhelmingly catch-up growth: importing Enlightenment-derived knowledge (markets, contract law, statistical methods, every layer of the tech stack from
  semiconductors to Linux) and applying it at scale to abundant labor and capital. The URM is a claim about open-ended progress at the frontier, not about who can run the playbook fastest. China's Total Factor
  Productivity has stagnated since roughly 2008; the easy gains from importing the URM's outputs are running out.

  2. The URM is operating in China — but domain-restricted. Recall the slide: the URM and anti-rational memes can coexist; what matters is where the URM operates explicitly. In Chinese battery chemistry, ML
  research, manufacturing process — yes, criticism and error correction run. In politics, history, social organization, public health origins, demographics — they don't. This is exactly what the talk predicts: a
  society can ride partial URM embodiment in narrow domains for a long time.

  3. The predictions land. Where criticism is forbidden, China fails systematically and at scale: Great Leap Forward (~30M dead), Cultural Revolution, one-child policy (now a demographic catastrophe), zero-COVID,
  Wuhan transparency, the real estate bubble (Evergrande/Country Garden), the regulatory whiplash that nuked the Ant IPO. The URM predicts these failure modes exactly — they're the domains where the URM was banned
   from operating.

  4. The Needham reversal. The interesting question isn't "Why is China rising now?" It's Joseph Needham's: "Why didn't China have a scientific revolution despite a 1,000-year technological lead?" The URM answers
  that cleanly — China had the inputs but never the meta-protocol. The current rise is the same society finally importing the protocol's outputs, not generating them.
